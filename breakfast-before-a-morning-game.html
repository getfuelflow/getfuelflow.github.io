Breakfast Before a Morning Game

Early games are tricky. You wake up, you're not hungry, and there's not much time. But playing on an empty stomach usually means running out of energy fast. Here's how to fuel for early starts.

Dinner the night before counts

For morning games, last night's dinner does a lot of the work. Have a normal, balanced dinner with a good portion of carbs, like pasta, rice, or potatoes, plus a protein and vegetables.

How early to eat

  • 2 to 3 hours before: a full breakfast.
  • 1 to 2 hours before: a smaller, lighter breakfast.
  • Less than 1 hour before: just a small, easy snack and some fluids.

Breakfast ideas (2 to 3 hours before)

  • Oatmeal with banana, honey, and milk
  • Eggs with toast and fruit
  • A bagel with peanut butter and a banana
  • Pancakes or waffles with fruit and yogurt
  • Greek yogurt with granola and berries

Lighter options (1 to 2 hours before)

  • Toast with honey or jam
  • A banana and a glass of milk
  • A granola bar and a yogurt
  • A smoothie with fruit and milk or yogurt

Not hungry or nervous?

Pre-game nerves can make eating hard. Try something you can drink, like a smoothie or chocolate milk, or small bites of simple foods like toast, a banana, or crackers. Something small is much better than nothing.

Tip: Set your alarm a little earlier so you have time to eat calmly instead of rushing out the door hungry.

Don't forget fluids

You wake up a little dehydrated after a night of sleep. Drink a glass of water with breakfast and bring a full bottle to the game.

What to avoid

  • Skipping breakfast completely
  • Greasy breakfast foods, like a big plate of bacon or hash browns, right before playing
  • Energy drinks or large amounts of coffee
  • Trying a new food on game day
