What to Eat Before a Tennis Match

Tennis is a stop-and-go sport: quick sprints, hard changes of direction, and matches that can last anywhere from one hour to three. What you eat before you step on court can be the difference between staying sharp in the third set and fading late. Here's a simple, practical guide.

3 to 4 hours before: a real meal

This is your main pre-match meal. Focus on carbohydrates, your body's main fuel for high-intensity play, with some protein, and keep fat and fiber moderate so it digests comfortably.

  • Rice or pasta with chicken and some vegetables
  • A turkey or chicken sandwich on whole-grain bread with fruit
  • Oatmeal with banana, honey, and a glass of milk (great for morning matches)
  • Roti or wraps with a lean protein and a side of rice
Tip: Stick to foods you've eaten before. Match day is not the day to try something new.

1 to 2 hours before: a light snack

If your meal was a while ago, top up with something small that's mostly carbs and easy on your stomach.

  • A banana or an apple
  • Toast or a bagel with a little jam or honey
  • Yogurt with fruit
  • A granola bar or a handful of crackers

30 to 60 minutes before: just a little

Keep it small. A few bites of banana, some dried fruit, or a few sips of a sports drink can give a quick boost without making you feel heavy during warm-up.

Hydration starts early

  • Drink water regularly in the hours before your match, not all at once right before.
  • Pale yellow urine is a simple sign you're well hydrated.
  • In hot weather, or if you sweat a lot, a sports drink or electrolyte mix helps replace the salt you lose.
  • Bring at least one full bottle on court and sip on every changeover.

During long matches

For matches lasting more than about an hour, small amounts of carbs on changeovers help keep your energy up: a few bites of banana, an energy chew, or sips of a sports drink. Keep drinking even if you don't feel thirsty.

Between matches at a tournament

Tournament days often mean two matches, or singles and doubles, in one day. Recovery between them matters as much as your pre-match meal.

  • Right after your match: drink fluids and have a snack with both carbs and protein, like chocolate milk, a yogurt and fruit, or a sandwich.
  • If you have 2 or more hours before the next match: eat a lighter version of your pre-match meal.
  • If you have less than an hour: stick to easy carbs and fluids.

What to avoid before playing

  • Big, greasy, or very spicy meals right before a match
  • Lots of fiber or beans close to match time
  • Energy drinks with large amounts of caffeine
  • Skipping meals. Playing on empty is one of the fastest ways to lose focus late in a match.
