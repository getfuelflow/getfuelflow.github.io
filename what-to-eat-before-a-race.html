What to Eat Before a Race

Whether it's a 5K, a 10K, or a half marathon, what you eat before race day can decide how your last mile feels. The longer the race, the more fueling matters. Here's a simple plan from the night before to the finish line.

The night before

Have a normal dinner with a good portion of carbs, like pasta, rice, or potatoes, plus some protein. Don't stuff yourself; a "carb overload" the night before a 5K usually just leaves you feeling heavy. For longer races, see Carb Loading Explained.

Race morning

Eat about 2 to 3 hours before the start. Choose easy-to-digest carbs with a little protein:

  • A bagel with peanut butter and a banana
  • Oatmeal with honey and fruit
  • Toast with jam and a yogurt
Tip: Practice your race breakfast before long training runs, so race morning feels familiar. Never try something new on race day.

30 to 60 minutes before the start

If you're hungry, have a small top-up: half a banana, a few crackers, or a few sips of a sports drink.

During the race

  • 5K and 10K: you usually don't need food. Water at an aid station is enough, especially if it's hot.
  • Half marathon and longer: most runners benefit from some carbs during the race, like energy gels, chews, or a sports drink. Practice with them in training first.
  • Drink small amounts at aid stations rather than gulping a whole cup.

Hydration

  • Drink water steadily the day before and the morning of the race.
  • Stop drinking large amounts about 30 to 45 minutes before the start to avoid a mid-race bathroom stop.
  • In hot weather, a sports drink helps replace the salt you sweat out.

After the finish

Grab fluids and a snack at the finish area, then eat a real meal with carbs and protein within a couple of hours. More ideas: What to Eat After a Workout.

What to avoid

  • High-fiber, greasy, or spicy foods the night before and morning of the race
  • Lots of caffeine if you're not used to it
  • New gels, drinks, or foods on race day
  • Skipping breakfast before a morning race
