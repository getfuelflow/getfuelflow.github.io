Carb Loading Explained

"Carb loading" is one of the most misunderstood ideas in sports nutrition. It doesn't mean eating a giant plate of pasta the night before every game. Here's what it really is, and when it actually helps.

What carb loading is

Your muscles store carbohydrate as an energy source called glycogen. Carb loading means eating more carbohydrates than usual for 1 to 3 days before a long event, while training less, so your muscles start the event with full energy stores.

Who it helps

  • Yes: long, continuous endurance events of about 90 minutes or more, like a half marathon, marathon, long-distance cycling, or triathlon.
  • Usually no: shorter events like a 5K or 10K, a single swim race, or most team sports games. A normal, carb-rich diet and a good pre-game meal are enough.
  • Long tournament weekends: no formal carb loading, but make sure every meal includes good carbs and you refuel between games.

How to do it simply

  • For the 1 to 3 days before the event, make carbohydrates the biggest part of every meal: rice, pasta, bread, potatoes, oats, fruit, and juice.
  • Keep protein moderate and cut back a little on fat and very high-fiber foods, which fill you up without adding much carbohydrate.
  • Train lightly or rest, so the extra carbs are stored instead of used.
  • Drink plenty of fluids. Your body stores water along with glycogen.
Note: You might feel slightly heavier after carb loading. That's normal: it's stored energy and water your muscles will use during the event.

Common mistakes

  • One huge meal the night before. Loading works over a couple of days, not in one overstuffed dinner, which can leave you uncomfortable on race morning.
  • Loading up on high-fat foods. Pizza and creamy sauces add fat, not much extra carbohydrate.
  • Too much fiber. Large salads, beans, and bran cereals the day before can upset your stomach mid-race.
  • Trying it for the first time on a big race. Practice before a long training run first.

Race morning still matters

Carb loading doesn't replace breakfast. Still have a familiar carb-based meal 2 to 3 hours before the start. More detail: What to Eat Before a Race.
