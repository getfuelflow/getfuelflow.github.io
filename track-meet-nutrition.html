Track Meet Nutrition

Track meets can last all day, with long waits between short, all-out efforts. Sprinters, jumpers, throwers, and distance runners all need fuel, just in different amounts. The goal: stay energized and hydrated without feeling heavy when your event is called.

Before the meet

Eat a meal 3 to 4 hours before your first event, mostly carbs with some protein:

  • Pasta or rice with chicken
  • A sandwich or wrap with fruit
  • For early meets: oatmeal, toast with peanut butter, or a bagel with a banana

Between events

  • Less than 30 minutes until your next event: water or a few sips of a sports drink.
  • 30 to 60 minutes: easy carbs, like a banana, pretzels, or an applesauce pouch.
  • 1 to 2 hours: a small snack with carbs and a little protein, like half a sandwich, yogurt, or a granola bar.
  • Long gaps (2+ hours): a light meal, then move to easier snacks as your event gets closer.

By event type

  • Sprints, hurdles, jumps, and throws: short efforts don't use huge amounts of fuel, but long meet days do. Small snacks keep your energy and focus steady while you wait.
  • Middle distance and distance: make sure your pre-meet meal has plenty of carbs, and avoid eating a big snack in the hour before your race.
  • Multi-event athletes: treat the day like a tournament. Eat and drink a little after every event.

Sun, heat, and hydration

  • Outdoor meets mean hours in the sun. Sip water constantly, not just when you feel thirsty.
  • A sports drink helps on hot days or if you're competing in multiple events.
  • Sit in the shade between events when you can, and wear sunscreen.

Pack a meet bag

  • Two water bottles and a sports drink
  • Bananas, oranges, or grapes
  • Pretzels, crackers, and granola bars
  • Sandwiches or wraps
  • Yogurt or chocolate milk in a small cooler

After the meet

Eat a meal with carbs and protein within about 1 to 2 hours. More ideas: What to Eat After a Workout.
