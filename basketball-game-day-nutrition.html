Basketball Game Day Nutrition

Basketball is fast and explosive: sprinting up and down the court, jumping, cutting, and defending with very little rest. Gyms are often warm, and weekend tournaments can mean three or four games in two days. Here's how to fuel so your legs are still there in the fourth quarter.

3 to 4 hours before tip-off: the pre-game meal

Make carbohydrates the main part of your meal, add some protein, and keep fat and fiber moderate so you don't feel heavy when you start running.

  • Pasta with chicken or meat sauce
  • A rice bowl with chicken or beef and a few vegetables
  • A turkey sandwich or wrap with fruit and pretzels
  • For morning games: eggs with toast and a banana, or oatmeal with fruit and milk
Tip: For a game right after school, lunch is your pre-game meal. Don't skip it, and pack a snack for the afternoon.

1 to 2 hours before: a light snack

  • A banana or an orange
  • A granola bar or a handful of pretzels
  • A bagel or toast with honey or jam
  • Yogurt with fruit

During the game

  • Timeouts and breaks: take a few sips of water every time you're on the bench.
  • Halftime: water or a sports drink, plus a few orange slices or a bite of banana if you're hungry.
  • Hot gyms: you sweat more than you think indoors. A sports drink helps replace lost salt, especially in long or overtime games.

Hydration

  • Start drinking water in the morning, not just at the game.
  • Bring your own water bottle so you're not relying on a gym fountain.
  • Muscle cramps, headaches, or feeling dizzy can be signs you need more fluid and salt.
  • Pale yellow urine is a simple sign you're well hydrated.

AAU and weekend tournaments

Tournament weekends often mean several games, long waits between them, and gym snack bars full of candy and nachos. Plan ahead:

  • Pack a cooler bag: sandwiches, wraps, fruit, yogurt, granola bars, pretzels, and plenty of drinks.
  • Right after each game: fluids plus a snack with carbs and protein, like chocolate milk, a sandwich, or yogurt and fruit.
  • 2 or more hours until the next game: a light meal, like a sandwich, fruit, and a drink.
  • Less than an hour: easy carbs and fluids only, like a banana and a sports drink.
  • Between days: a good dinner with carbs and protein, plenty of water, and a full night of sleep are the best way to bounce back for day two.

After the game

Within about 1 to 2 hours, eat a meal with carbs and protein to refill your energy and help your muscles recover. For more ideas, see What to Eat After a Workout.

What to avoid on game day

  • Fast food, fried foods, or heavy meals before playing
  • Candy and soda as your main fuel at tournaments. They give a quick spike, then a crash.
  • Energy drinks with lots of caffeine
  • Trying new foods or supplements on game day
