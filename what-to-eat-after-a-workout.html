What to Eat After a Workout

Whether it's practice, a game, a run, or a gym session, what you eat afterward helps your body recover and get ready for the next one. Here's what to eat, when to eat it, and what to skip.

Why eating after a workout matters

  • Refill your energy. Hard training uses up the carbohydrates stored in your muscles. Eating carbs afterward refills them.
  • Repair your muscles. Protein gives your muscles what they need to recover and get stronger.
  • Replace fluids. You lose water and salt through sweat, especially in hot weather or long sessions.

When to eat

Try to eat a snack or a meal within about 1 to 2 hours after training. If you're training or competing again later the same day, eat sooner, ideally within 30 to 60 minutes, so you're ready for the next session.

Tip: If you're not hungry right after a hard session, start with something you can drink, like chocolate milk or a smoothie, then eat a full meal later.

What to eat: carbs + protein

The best recovery snacks and meals combine carbohydrates to refill your energy and protein to rebuild your muscles. A simple guide for protein: about a palm-sized portion.

Quick snacks:

  • Chocolate milk
  • Greek yogurt with fruit and granola
  • A smoothie with milk, a banana, and peanut butter
  • A turkey or chicken sandwich
  • Cheese and crackers with an apple

Full meals:

  • Rice bowl with chicken, vegetables, and a sauce you like
  • Pasta with meat sauce or chicken
  • Eggs, toast, and fruit
  • Wraps or roti with a protein and a side of rice
  • Plant-based: tofu or beans with rice, or lentil soup with bread

Rehydrate

  • Drink water steadily after training, not all at once.
  • If you sweated a lot or trained in the heat, a sports drink or salty snack helps replace lost electrolytes.
  • Pale yellow urine is a simple sign you're well hydrated again.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping food after evening practice. Your body still needs to recover, even if it's late. A small snack or light meal is better than nothing.
  • Only having protein. A protein shake alone skips the carbs your muscles need to refill their energy.
  • Relying on supplements. Most athletes can get everything they need from real food. Talk to a doctor or dietitian before adding supplements, especially if you're a teen.
  • Forgetting fluids. Feeling tired or getting headaches after training can be a sign you didn't drink enough.
