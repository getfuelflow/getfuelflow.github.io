Soccer Game Day Nutrition

Soccer means running for most of 90 minutes, with sprints, tackles, and only one real break at halftime. Players can cover several miles in a single game, so running low on fuel shows up fast, especially in the second half. Here's how to eat so you finish as strong as you start.

The night before

Game day fueling starts the evening before. Have a normal, balanced dinner with a good portion of carbohydrates, like pasta, rice, or potatoes, plus a protein and some vegetables. No need to overeat, just don't skip it.

3 to 4 hours before kickoff: the pre-game meal

Your main meal should be rich in carbohydrates, with some protein, and fairly low in fat and fiber so it digests well before you run.

  • Pasta with a tomato or meat sauce
  • Rice with chicken or fish and a few vegetables
  • A sandwich or wrap with lean meat, plus fruit
  • For morning games: pancakes or toast with eggs and a banana, or oatmeal with fruit
Tip: Early game? Eat a bigger dinner the night before and a lighter breakfast you know sits well, at least 2 hours before kickoff.

1 to 2 hours before: a small top-up

  • A banana or a handful of grapes
  • A granola bar or pretzels
  • Toast or a bagel with honey
  • Applesauce pouch

Halftime: quick fuel

Halftime is your one chance to refuel during the game. Keep it fast and easy to digest:

  • Orange slices (a soccer classic for a reason: carbs and fluid in one)
  • A few bites of banana
  • Sips of a sports drink, especially in warm weather
  • Water, even if you don't feel thirsty

Hydration

  • Drink water steadily throughout game day, starting in the morning.
  • Take sips during warm-up and every chance you get during stoppages.
  • In hot weather or long games, a sports drink helps replace the salt you sweat out.
  • Cramps late in games can be a sign you didn't drink or eat enough.

Weekend tournaments: multiple games a day

Tournaments can mean two or three games in one day. Pack your own food so you're not stuck with whatever the snack stand sells.

  • Pack a cooler: sandwiches, fruit, yogurt, pretzels, granola bars, and plenty of drinks.
  • Right after each game: fluids plus a snack with carbs and protein, like chocolate milk or a sandwich.
  • 2+ hours until the next game: a light meal, like a sandwich and fruit.
  • Less than an hour: easy carbs and fluids only.

After the final whistle

Within about 1 to 2 hours after the game, eat a meal with carbs and protein to refill your energy and help your muscles recover, like a rice bowl with chicken, pasta, or eggs and toast. For more ideas, see What to Eat After a Workout.

What to avoid on game day

  • Fast food or fried meals before the game
  • Big portions of beans, broccoli, or other high-fiber foods close to kickoff
  • Energy drinks with lots of caffeine
  • Trying a new food or supplement for the first time on game day
