How Much Water Should Athletes Drink?

Even mild dehydration can make you feel tired, slow your reactions, and hurt your performance, especially in the heat. The good news: staying hydrated is mostly about simple habits, not exact numbers.

Every day

  • Carry a water bottle and sip throughout the day, not just at practice.
  • Drink with every meal.
  • Check your urine color: pale yellow usually means you're well hydrated. Dark yellow means drink more.
  • Thirst is a late signal. If you feel very thirsty, you're probably already behind.

Before training or competing

Drink water in the few hours before you play, then have a few more sips about 15 to 30 minutes before you start. Avoid chugging a huge amount right before, which can leave you feeling sloshy.

During training or competing

  • Take regular sips at every break, timeout, or changeover.
  • For sessions over about an hour, or in the heat, a sports drink helps replace both fluid and the salt you lose in sweat.
  • Some athletes sweat a lot more than others. If you finish practice with white, salty streaks on your clothes, you're a "salty sweater" and may benefit from electrolytes.

After training

Keep drinking over the next few hours to replace what you lost, and eat a meal or snack, since food also contains water and salt. More on recovery: What to Eat After a Workout.

Signs you need more fluid

  • Headache or feeling dizzy
  • Muscle cramps
  • Unusual tiredness or trouble focusing
  • Dark yellow urine
Safety: In extreme heat, stop and get help if you feel confused, very dizzy, stop sweating, or feel sick. Heat illness is serious. Tell a coach or adult right away.

Can you drink too much?

Yes, though it's rare. Drinking huge amounts of plain water during very long events, without any salt, can lower the sodium in your blood. For long or hot sessions, mix in sports drinks or salty snacks, and drink to stay comfortable rather than forcing as much as possible.

What about energy drinks?

Energy drinks aren't sports drinks. Many contain large amounts of caffeine and sugar and aren't recommended for teens. Stick to water and, when needed, sports drinks.
